# Concept 09: Bloom & Field

[Homepage](../../prototypes/bloom.html) | [Store preview](../../prototypes/store.html?design=bloom) | [Project plan](../../prototypes/plan.html?doc=design09)

## Thesis and visual system

A botanical seed catalogue reinterpreted for hive goods: framed collection plates, layered labels and practical material notes make discovery feel curious and considered. Products lead; botanical imagery does not imply a formula, honey origin or environmental credential.

- Paper `#f7f5e9`, ink `#2e4938`, berry `#7b4159`, pink `#ead9db`.
- Georgia/Times New Roman system serif for display; Arial/Helvetica for body and catalogue labels.
- Fine double frames, rectangular seed-packet product cards, flowering-branch/bee SVG, small numbered material notes. No meadow arch, availability calendar or ornamental carousel.

## Products, pickup and commerce

The hero opens `store.html?design=bloom`; card titles and art open the actual seasonal-honey, honey-oat-soap and beeswax-candle previews. The shared store/product/cart/inventory flow keeps `design=bloom`. Homepage goods are conceptual, with no price, quantity or purchase affordance.

Soap is the near-term planning priority; the sample honey-and-oat formula is not an approved SKU. Consumer honey remains a future retail concept with no real stock, preorders or harvest promise, and candles remain planned. Production quantities and commercial commitments belong only in the internal hub, not public-facing story copy.

Pickup comes first and shipping later, but no public address, pickup hours, ready-by time or availability is invented. Honey allocation, formulas and candle readiness remain open. Property removal and farm pollination are visibly separate, scoped conversations, with contact previews rather than booking. Direct contact and business-owned Facebook/Instagram URLs are pending.

## Acquisition hypothesis and future SEO

The [existing research](../01-market-and-design-research.md) observes material/origin stories at Appalachian Bee and Big Island Bees and educational merchandising at larger retailers. Hypothesis: useful material questions can help local soap buyers and approved social referrals compare categories. The research does not support a conversion percentage, a health benefit or an eco claim.

Future SEO should connect approved product pages to accurate ingredients/materials, photos, formats and pickup information, plus true Madisonville identity. Material notes should become useful factual copy only after owner review, not thin keyword articles or fabricated bloom/harvest schedules. Separate real service pages should match approved capability. Follow the [shared acquisition plan](../06-storefront-seo-and-acquisition.md); review pages stay noindex without offer/rating markup.

## Mobile behavior

Below 650px the shop copy comes first, the framed art follows, and catalogue cards become full-width. Material notes, story and service panels stack. The story label compresses into a short horizontal heading, preserving content without dominating the screen. Visible navigation, large primary controls, no homepage JavaScript and shared reduced-motion handling remain intact.

## Tradeoffs and owner decisions

Botanical art may suggest ingredients or environmental claims if captions disappear; preserve the explicit illustration/origin disclaimer. The catalogue framing adds visual density and may feel less direct than the minimal concepts. A real collection will need consistent imagery and accurate labels, not just appealing flowers.

Confirm real formulas, origin wording, honey retail allocation, candle materials/safety, owner story, pickup process, contact/profile URLs and service scope/capacity. Decide whether educational content can be maintained. Validate product versus service discovery with the same tasks used for other concepts.
