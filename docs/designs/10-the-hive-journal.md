# Concept 10: The Hive Journal

[Homepage](../../prototypes/journal.html) | [Store preview](../../prototypes/store.html?design=journal) | [Project plan](../../prototypes/plan.html?doc=design10)

## Thesis and visual system

An editorial front page for a local product business: a broad masthead, a lead product story, a contents rail and useful product briefs invite browsing without obscuring shopping. The journal is a design language, not an invented publication archive or founding history.

- Paper `#f6f1e7`, ink `#302c29`, rust `#8f422c`, muted cream `#e9dfce`.
- Georgia/Times New Roman system serif for masthead, display and reading text; Arial/Helvetica for navigation, labels, captions and controls.
- Double rules, newspaper columns, a drop cap, a non-attributed editorial pull statement and original engraving-style crosshatched SVG. Issue labels explicitly say design edition/concept, not a fake founding year.

## Shopping, pickup and service paths

The hero opens `store.html?design=journal`; the contents rail and product briefs link directly to the shared seasonal-honey, honey-oat-soap and beeswax-candle previews. The store/product/cart/inventory flow keeps `design=journal`. The homepage has no stock, prices or purchase buttons; it makes product exploration unmistakable without pretending a retail honey release exists.

Soap is the near-term planning priority; the sample honey-and-oat formula is not an approved SKU. Consumer honey remains a future retail concept with no real stock, preorders or harvest promise, and candles remain planned. Production quantities and commercial commitments belong only in the internal hub, not public-facing story copy.

The local note says pickup first, shipping later, with actual location/timing/process still pending. Removal and farm pollination are separate service notices with assessment and capacity caveats. Contact is a truthful preview; direct phone/email and verified Facebook/Instagram URLs are pending. Nothing submits an inquiry or booking.

## Acquisition hypothesis and future SEO

The [existing research](../01-market-and-design-research.md) observes provenance/making content and product education across regional and national analogues. Hypothesis: useful short product briefs may help word-of-mouth and approved social visitors understand a small maker range and continue to a real product page. Newspaper styling is not evidence of trust, authority, readership or sales performance.

Future SEO should prioritize accurate approved collection/product pages, not an obligatory blog. Publish material/process stories only when genuinely useful and maintainable; no fabricated historical posts. Explain actual pickup expectations and Madisonville identity, and create separate real service pages only for approved work. Follow the [shared acquisition plan](../06-storefront-seo-and-acquisition.md); demo pages remain noindex with no offer schema.

## Mobile behavior

Below 1050px the contents rail becomes a lower three-part index. Below 650px it stacks beneath the lead image, as do product, story and service columns. Masthead lettering scales without truncation; navigation remains visible. Product briefs preserve title, art, short description and a large text-link target. No homepage JavaScript, ticker or pagination illusion; shared reduced-motion rules remain active.

## Tradeoffs and owner decisions

Editorial texture adds reading and may slow an immediate product-selection task. Shop actions and linked contents must remain stronger than decorative "journal" cues. It also creates a maintenance expectation; the owner can retain the composition without committing to a blog. Engraving art is distinctive but must not substitute for real product information.

Confirm approved products/formulas, honey allocation, candle readiness, real imagery, owner story, pickup process, contact/profile URLs and service capability. Decide whether editorial content is sustainable. Compare task completion with more direct retail directions rather than treating a rich masthead as proof of trust.
