# Concept 02: Fieldwork

[Open prototype](../../prototypes/fieldwork.html) | [Store preview](../../prototypes/store.html?design=fieldwork) | [Planning hub](../../prototypes/plan.html?doc=design02)

## Design thesis

Beekeeping is practical work for real places. A clear, capable service-led page should help a homeowner or grower immediately recognize the appropriate conversation.

**Strongest pure service-launch hypothesis.** Use this if removal/pollination inquiries matter much more than near-term product branding. It can also supply the service hierarchy within Meadow & Amber.

The owner now prioritizes products/local sales. Fieldwork is therefore a **service-forward comparison reference**, not the default recommendation; its hero still provides a direct coordinated-store route.

| Shape | Square modules, purposeful dividers, numbered process |
| Imagery | Original isometric apiary/site study; actual service/keeper photographs later |

The field illustration is explicitly **not a geographic map**. Numbers organize steps/services; they are not fake performance metrics, years of experience or hive counts.

Compared with Meadow & Amber, service cards dominate the page and contain more practical scoping information. Compared with Hive & Hearth, product imagery is deliberately secondary.

## Responsive and interaction rules

Cards stack at mobile widths; each service retains a full-width next-step button and its scope caveat. The process becomes a vertical sequence. Navigation wraps rather than disappearing into an unimplemented menu. Use visible focus, generous targets and no JavaScript-dependent primary content.

No form or appointment system exists in the prototype. Its inquiry panel explains what the future conversation needs. Facebook/Instagram are pending labels, not guessed destinations.

Home products lead to the coordinated collection/product/cart/checkout preview, with the same bounded fictional inventory behavior as the other directions. No real purchase, reservation or retail honey release is implied.

## Evidence and limits

UT Extension's swarm-call guidance and the archived UF/IFAS pollination example demonstrate that the two services need different information. Big Island Bees shows distinct visitor-intent routing in its content. These support the **information architecture**, not a claim that green grids or numbered cards convert better. See [research](../01-market-and-design-research.md).

## Tradeoffs

| Advantage | Risk / mitigation |
|---|---|
| Clearest homeowner/grower routing | Can feel institutional; add real keeper/work imagery and neighborly language |
| Service scope and limitations are visible | Long caveats can dominate; owner approval should make final copy concise and accurate |
| Easy to extend into service detail pages | Do not turn farm inquiries into simplistic instant hive bookings |
| Strong mobile task focus | Keep the local product story visible enough to support future retail |

## Production translation and decision

Reuse shared content/cards/layout boundaries; do not introduce a separate service app. Add detailed service routes only when phase 3 has useful owner-approved content. Keep retail stock and service capacity separate.

Evaluate with the [shared rubric](01-meadow-and-amber.md#shared-evaluation-rubric). Favor this direction if real task review shows a meaningful service-comprehension advantage and the owner likes the working-apiary identity.

**Approval needed:** actual service categories/coverage, owner process, contact/profile URLs, imagery and service-versus-product business priorities.

## Future SEO intent

Service pages require real capability and distinct useful information; product pages need actual soap/format/price/pickup facts. The live site must expose crawlable collection/product links under the chosen primary-domain route strategy. This review stays noindex and does not publish mock offers or pretend local service coverage.
