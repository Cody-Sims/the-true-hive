# Concept 04: Apiary Market

[Open homepage](../../prototypes/market.html) | [Store preview](../../prototypes/store.html?design=market) | [Project and business plan](../../prototypes/plan.html) | [Comparison gallery](../../prototypes/index.html)

## Thesis

Make The True Hive feel like a small, well-arranged artisan market: easy to
understand, warm enough to approach, and useful before it is decorative. Honey,
soap, and candles lead. Local pickup explains how a future sale would work.
Colony removal and farm pollination remain distinct, visible secondary paths.

This is a product-discovery hypothesis, not a claim that the business operates a
market stand or has approved this identity. The stand is original illustrative
art; it is not an event listing, actual location, or product photograph.

## Owner context: planning-only boundary

Owner-supplied operational context is not approved public marketing. Soap is the
most grounded near-term product; candles remain planned. Consumer honey is a
future retail concept, with no real stock, preorder, or harvest allocation
offered. Future harvest or B2B commitments do not establish consumer retail
inventory. Do not turn operational scale, production ownership, or partnership
context into homepage claims without separate approval.

## Palette, type, and graphic language

| Role | Choice and application |
|---|---|
| Paper | `#fff7e8`, the main page and product-label ground |
| Forest | `#233b30`, body ink, structural rules, shelf edge, pickup board |
| Tomato | `#a63226`, primary action, display accent, service dividers |
| Ochre | `#f4cd60`, collection ribbon and ticket; always dark text |
| Utility display | Arial Black/Arial/system sans, tightly set, heavy uppercase |
| Editorial display | Georgia/system serif for supporting headings and the hero's final word |
| Body | Arial/Helvetica/system sans, locally available; no font requests |
| Motifs | Rectangular shelf, numbered paper tickets, striped stand, very restrained gingham |

This deliberately avoids Meadow's arch and Hearth's premium-apothecary cues.
Tickets identify a category, not an invented price, discount, or inventory count.
The original stand groups different goods into a recognizable retail scene.

Calculated solid-color text pair ratios: forest/paper approximately **11.34:1**,
tomato/paper **6.35:1**, forest/ochre **7.90:1**. These measurements do not replace
whole-page accessibility testing.

## Customer acquisition rationale: a testable hypothesis

The [existing research](../01-market-and-design-research.md) records local
identity/contact patterns in the Madisonville producer listing and a
product-family/making-story pattern at Appalachian Bee. These are content
observations, not conversion studies or proof of local demand.

**Hypothesis:** a local shopper arriving from a verified social profile, an
owner-shared link, packaging, or a future market conversation will quickly
recognize the product families and understand the pickup-first model. The
friendly shelf may make modest everyday purchases feel more approachable than
a luxury gift-store treatment.

Test the shared rubric in
[Meadow & Amber](01-meadow-and-amber.md#shared-evaluation-rubric). In addition,
ask a local soap buyer to locate the soap preview and explain whether they just
placed an order. Ask another reviewer whether the illustration implies a public
market location. Any confusion about orders or a real stall is a failure to fix,
not a desirable sense of realism. No uplift, audience size, or revenue is assumed.

## Coordinated collection-to-cart journey

The shared query parameter **`design=market`** identifies this theme throughout
the commerce review.

1. The primary hero action and footer enter the
   [complete store](../../prototypes/store.html?design=market).
2. The shelf links to full product previews:
   [seasonal honey](../../prototypes/product.html?design=market&product=seasonal-honey),
   [honey + oat soap](../../prototypes/product.html?design=market&product=honey-oat-soap),
   and [beeswax candle](../../prototypes/product.html?design=market&product=beeswax-candle).
   Product details and variant selection belong on those shared pages, not in
   pretend homepage controls.
3. The shared store/product experience continues to the
   [sample cart](../../prototypes/cart.html?design=market), preserving the theme.
   Fictional prices and stock must remain explicitly demo data. Nothing is paid,
   reserved, submitted, or ordered.
4. The owner-facing
   [inventory preview](../../prototypes/inventory.html?design=market) is a review
   tool, not a public inventory source or production admin system.

The real launch requires confirmed products, availability, pricing, payment
handling, and private pickup arrangements. Shipping is a later operational
decision, not a functioning service in this concept.

## Mobile, accessibility, and resilience

- At wide widths the stand sits beside the product-led hero; the shelf has three
  equal columns, and the pickup board has two columns.
- At 760px and below, the hero and pickup board stack, keeping copy and its store
  action before the art. Shelf entries become compact side-by-side rows.
- At 480px and below, each shelf entry becomes an image-over-copy card; service
  and contact sections stack. Product links remain visible without hover.
- Navigation and primary links wrap rather than hiding behind a script-driven
  menu. Key controls are at least 44 CSS px tall, with visible keyboard focus.
- One h1, a skip link, labeled navigation, real anchor destinations, fixed image
  dimensions, and HTML-first content support keyboard and no-JavaScript use.
- There is no animation, carousel, third-party embed, remote font, homepage
  script, or external image request. Reduced motion disables smooth scrolling.
- Phone-width review should include 320/375px, tablet 768px, desktop 1440px, zoom,
  keyboard order, and checks that decorative rotated tickets never create
  horizontal page overflow.

## Future SEO intent

Keep this review **noindex**. If selected, produce one canonical public homepage
with actual honey/soap/candle collection and product pages, useful product
descriptions, accurate Madisonville context, and verified contact information.
Separate removal and farm-pollination pages should explain real coverage and
assessment scope, rather than becoming unrelated keywords in product copy.

Do not publish a fake pickup address, opening hours, offer/review schema,
availability, certification, or medicinal claim. Structured data and search
metadata must reflect approved, genuinely available goods and real business
facts. No search ranking or rich-result outcome is promised.

## Tradeoffs and mitigations

| Opportunity | Tradeoff / mitigation |
|---|---|
| Immediately legible small-retail identity | The stand can imply an actual stall; retain the illustrative caption and avoid event/place claims |
| Friendly and practical rather than precious | Utility typography can get loud; keep body copy quiet and preserve generous shelf spacing |
| Clear pickup sequence | A long process can feel bureaucratic; keep three short steps and confirm the actual handoff model |
| Services remain findable without leading | Service visitors scroll farther; retain top navigation and independent service destinations |
| Strong shelf metaphor translates into catalog | Avoid excessive ornamental tickets, promotional clutter, or invented prices |

## Open owner decisions

Approve or reject the market tone; confirm actual soap range/formulas, whether
any honey can enter a separately approved retail catalog, planned candle
materials, sizes, packaging, pricing, and stock. Decide private
pickup arrangements and payment process before publishing any sales CTA.
Supply the real owner story, photographs, phone/email, and owned social URLs.
Confirm removal coverage/capability, structural-work and repair boundaries,
pollination capacity, and service-intake process. Select a direction using local
tasks, not just palette preference. Shipping policies and public visits remain
unapproved.
