# Concept 07: Golden Hour

[Homepage](../../prototypes/golden.html) | [Store preview](../../prototypes/store.html?design=golden) | [Project plan](../../prototypes/plan.html?doc=design07)

## Thesis and visual system

A sunlit maker's shelf: oversized, friendly type and ceramic warmth make useful goods the first thing a local shopper sees. This is a product-brand hypothesis, not a claim that the pictured range is ready.

- Paper `#fff5ed`, ink `#5c3229`, terracotta `#b04b2d`, apricot `#f0c4a0`.
- Arial/Helvetica system sans for display and body; Georgia for italic category accents and the illustrative monogram. No downloaded fonts.
- Asymmetric rectangular still life, circular sunlight, woven cloth and a pottery-like platter. Original SVG art is explicitly conceptual.

## Product, pickup and service paths

The hero opens `store.html?design=golden`. The ribbon and product-card art/titles open actual shared product previews for seasonal honey, honey-and-oat soap and beeswax candle. No homepage price, stock or purchase button implies availability. Shared collection, product, cart and inventory tools retain the `golden` design context; their commerce is fictional and cannot create orders.

Soap is the near-term planning priority; the sample honey-and-oat formula is not an approved SKU. Consumer honey remains a future retail concept with no real stock, preorders or harvest promise, and candles remain planned. Production quantities and commercial commitments belong only in the internal hub, not public-facing story copy.

Local pickup is the intended first fulfillment model; shipping is later. Location, timing and eligibility are deliberately unspecified. A distinct terracotta chapter separates property removal from grower pollination, with assessment/capacity caveats and contact-preview routes, not booking promises. Owner words, real photographs, direct contact and verified social URLs remain pending.

## Acquisition hypothesis and future SEO

The [existing research](../01-market-and-design-research.md) observes product-family navigation and making/origin stories at Appalachian Bee and Big Island Bees. Hypothesis: a cohesive everyday-goods shelf could help current soap buyers or approved social referrals explore an additional category. It does not establish a conversion lift, local price point or demand.

Future approved product/collection pages should carry actual materials, formats, availability and pickup expectations, with useful Madisonville context. Distinct real service pages should match approved scope. Follow the [acquisition plan](../06-storefront-seo-and-acquisition.md); this review remains noindex with no offer schema.

## Mobile behavior

The headline and shop action precede the art. Below 650px the category ribbon becomes three full-width rows, cards lose their stagger, and pickup/story/service/contact grids stack. No hidden menu, carousel or homepage JavaScript. Controls remain visible; shared reduced-motion rules disable smooth scrolling.

## Tradeoffs and owner decisions

Warm art and playful typography may suit home goods better than urgent property inquiries; the always-visible services navigation mitigates that hierarchy. Real product photography must eventually replace illustrative packaging without losing warmth. The tall mobile page favors comprehension over compression.

Decide the approved real soap range, honey retail allocation, candle readiness, actual packaging, pickup process, contact/profile URLs, service scope and owner story. Test shop discovery and service discovery against the same tasks as other concepts; do not select on palette alone.
