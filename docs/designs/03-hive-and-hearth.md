# Concept 03: Hive & Hearth

[Open prototype](../../prototypes/hearth.html) | [Store preview](../../prototypes/store.html?design=hearth) | [Planning hub](../../prototypes/plan.html?doc=design03)

## Design thesis

Honey, soap and candles can share a tactile, considered "hive-to-home" identity. This direction explores that future brand without pretending an online shop already exists.

**Product-brand hypothesis** that matches the clarified local-product priority, subject to owner/customer review. It does not establish a premium price point or actual product readiness.

## Visual system

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Plum ink | `#4a304a` |
| Ivory | `#fbf8f2` |
| Blush background | `#eee5eb` |
| Secondary text | `#705e6a` |
| Type | Refined system serif display, restrained sans labels/body |
| Shape | Quiet rectangular image fields, delicate rules, original wordmark seal |
| Imagery | Sculptural original still-life illustration now; consistent real product/making photographs later |

No health/supplement positioning, medical promises, invented luxury credentials, artificial scarcity or faux reviews.

The service link is visible in the hero and navigation even though goods lead the page. Services receive complete distinct rows later, rather than a tiny footer mention.

## Responsive and interaction rules

Mobile places the headline, collection action and beekeeper shortcut before the still life. Product cards become single-column; service rows reflow to number/content/action. Header navigation stays visible and has distinct accessible labels.

The homepage links to its coordinated store, product/variant, demo cart and read-only checkout handoff. Every sample price/count is fictional; no payment/order or honey retail release occurs. Social destinations are pending and contact is explanatory rather than an inactive form. No fake feed, reviews, shipping threshold or newsletter collection.

## Evidence and limits

Appalachian Bee connects honey to soaps/body goods, and Beekeeper's Naturals uses ingredient education to organize product exploration. These are **merchandising patterns**, not support for supplement claims or proof of a premium local price point. The plum palette and still-life art direction are original proposals. See [research](../01-market-and-design-research.md).

## Tradeoffs

| Advantage | Risk / mitigation |
|---|---|
| Strong foundation for giftable goods and future packaging | May imply shopping is available; preserve explicit sample/no-purchase labels |
| Distinct from conventional yellow/black bee branding | Need actual customer/owner feedback; taste is not market proof |
| Calm material-focused storytelling | Requires good real photography and accurate ingredient/sourcing information |
| Service support under one identity | Service content is lower; hero shortcut and clear nav are mandatory |

## Production translation and decision

Use the same small architecture, typed product states and service contracts as the other concepts. A premium visual direction does not require headless commerce, custom animations or a custom admin. When selling starts, native Shopify remains a valid implementation.

Evaluate with the [shared rubric](01-meadow-and-amber.md#shared-evaluation-rubric). Choose this only if product-brand fit and actual customer tasks justify the service-discovery tradeoff.

**Approval needed:** long-term product positioning, real formulas/claims/photography, packaging direction, contact/profile URLs and the prominence of services.

## Future SEO intent

Build useful real collection/product pages for approved products, with visible details and truthful primary-domain metadata/availability. Do not substitute polished mock packaging for actual photos/labels or publish sample offers. The owner-approved public preview remains noindex; confidential future plans and real customer data are not included.
