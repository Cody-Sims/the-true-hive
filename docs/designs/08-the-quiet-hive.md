# Concept 08: The Quiet Hive

## Thesis and visual system

Make a small local collection feel clear rather than small: a precise Swiss-inspired grid, large left-aligned typography and honest product specifications replace decoration and urgency. Minimal means complete information with deliberate hierarchy, not an empty interface.

- Paper `#f6f5f0`, ink `#1c2824`, sage `#53685b`, pale gray `#dfe4dc`.
- Arial/Helvetica system sans for display and body; Courier New for figure/index details.
- Square `t/h` monogram, fine rules, object-study SVG with alignment marks, no rounded cards or ornamental landscape.

## Products, local pickup and commerce

The hero shop action opens `store.html?design=quiet`. Each substantial product row links to the shared product detail using the correct product ID. Collection, product, demo cart and read-only inventory keep the same design context. The homepage contains no buy buttons, sample prices or quantities. The collection, formulas, honey release and candle composition remain provisional.

Soap is the near-term planning priority; the sample honey-and-oat formula is not an approved SKU. Consumer honey remains a future retail concept with no real stock, preorders or harvest promise, and candles remain planned. Production quantities and commercial commitments belong only in the internal hub, not public-facing story copy.

Pickup-first framing appears beside the hero action and in the local-context strip. It is not a location/hours, free-pickup or ready-now promise; shipping is a later operational decision. Removal and farm pollination are separate service summaries with assessment caveats and honest contact status. Direct phone/email and social destinations are pending, not disabled forms or guessed URLs.

## Acquisition hypothesis and future SEO

The [existing research](../01-market-and-design-research.md) observes recognizable product categories and useful local contact information. Hypothesis: current soap buyers and mobile referrals may value fast product identification and clear pickup expectations more than a heavily atmospheric landing page. There is no measured conversion or local demand claim.

Future SEO should use real collection/product pages with accurate formula/materials, format, photos and fulfillment information. Madisonville identity is explicit without a fabricated public store. Separate approved service pages can serve removal versus pollination intent. Follow the [shared acquisition plan](../06-storefront-seo-and-acquisition.md); noindex review pages are not search landing pages.

## Mobile behavior

At 650px the contextual rail moves above its content, hero statement stays large, and product rows become image/brief/arrow blocks. The large image becomes an inspectable product figure rather than a tiny three-column thumbnail. Navigation remains visible. Story and service columns stack; contact status labels sit above values. No homepage JavaScript or motion is needed; shared reduced-motion handling remains active.

## Tradeoffs and owner decisions

Precise restraint may feel less handmade or gift-oriented than warmer alternatives. The information density is low at the top and higher in the product index; test whether shoppers discover all three categories and services. Real photography must be unusually consistent for the restrained layout to hold together.

Confirm approved products/formulas, honey allocation, candle readiness, real imagery, owner story, pickup process, contact/profile URLs and service capability/coverage. Test tone with existing buyers rather than inferring a premium audience from minimal styling.
