# Business plan: start locally, earn the right to grow

Status: **working business plan, not a financial forecast or legal opinion**.  
Date: **2026-10-28**. Audience: the owners and business partner; current snapshot approved for public planning review, not final consumer marketing.

## Starting position: what the owner actually confirmed

| Confirmed in conversation | Planning implication |
|---|---|
| Already keeping bees, with two hives | This is not a hypothetical apiary, but commercial capacity and harvest outcomes are not established. |
| Already selling soaps on a small scale | Soap is the most grounded near-term retail offer. Start by improving this existing operation, not inventing an entirely new catalog. |
| Honey and products are intended to be their own production | Cost, batch, labeling, production time and quality control must reflect actual making, not a reseller model. |
| Next year's honey has a purchaser through a meadery partnership | Treat the 2027 harvest as allocated for planning unless the owners decide otherwise. Do not promise retail honey, preorders, or unallocated quantities. Agreement details and contingencies remain unverified. |
| Local product sales are the primary website priority | Product discovery, accurate product information and a dependable local purchase/pickup journey lead the experience. |
| Local pickup first, shipping later | Resolve a safe lawful pickup arrangement and readiness notifications before shipping complexity. |
| Candles, other hive goods, removal and pollination are future offerings | They are opportunities to evaluate, not proven production capacity or immediately bookable services. |

These are **owner-reported facts**, not independently audited records. The owner approved this current top-level context for public planning review. Buyer identity, confidential commercial terms and future private details are not supplied or approved. The meadery relationship does not make this a plan to produce or sell alcoholic drinks.

## Business thesis

Build a trusted Madisonville-based maker business around a small range of repeatable, honestly described soaps and later approved hive-related products. Operate the apiary and existing honey-supply relationship responsibly. Use the website to make local discovery, product understanding and pickup straightforward; add broader retail and services only after capacity, compliance and contribution margins support them.

The immediate opportunity is **better execution of the business that already exists**: consistent soap records, good photographs, known costs, reliable batches, clear pickup and customer follow-through. A large catalog, ten production websites, nationwide shipping and an elaborate custom admin are not launch requirements.

## B0 - Establish the operating foundation

**Purpose:** make expansion intentional and clarify the partners' responsibilities.

**Actions:** record ownership/roles and decision rights; review existing entity/trade-name, registration, tax and local-license status; confirm business account ownership, records/bookkeeping and appropriate insurance. Classify each soap/product using its actual ingredients and claims. Confirm apiary registration and other applicable requirements with current official/local guidance.

**Inputs:** current records, product formulas/labels, existing sales channels, owner/partner roles. No private account credentials belong in this repository.

**Acceptance:** a simple responsibility matrix, required registrations/confirmations, insurer/adviser contacts held privately, and a documented list of unresolved obligations. Do not assume an LLC is mandatory or that a home-production exemption applies to every product or channel.

**Gate:** complete the applicable confirmations before expanding into a new product, public pickup arrangement, wholesale commitment, or sales channel. Current operations have not been legally audited by this project.

**Partner questions:** Q01-Q05, Q11.

## B1 - Make the existing soap range repeatable

**Purpose:** understand what can be made and sold consistently before increasing demand.

**Actions:** select a small initial range based on actual sales and production ability; assign product/variant/SKU records; document formula/version, batch/lot, ingredients, supplier lots, date, process/quality checks, finished quantity and approved label. Record materials, packaging, direct labor, waste and payment costs. Obtain consistent real product photos and approved product descriptions.

Do not silently substitute the prototype's honey-and-oat formula, invented weights or sample prices for the real soaps. Do not add therapeutic, antibacterial, allergy, organic or other claims without the relevant evidence and regulatory review.

**Acceptance:** the owner can reproduce a batch, trace it to materials, count finished sellable units, explain the real label, and calculate contribution per unit. Product complaints can be linked back to a batch.

**Gate:** no new retail SKU until its product facts, label, batch record, cost, physical stock and quality checks are ready.

**Partner questions:** Q02, Q06, Q11-Q13.

## B2 - Prove a dependable local sales loop

**Purpose:** earn repeat customers through reliable fulfillment rather than broad advertising.

**Actions:** talk with existing soap buyers about purchase reason, scent/format preferences, repeat cadence and pickup friction; test approved local markets or a small number of local outlets; agree a safe pickup process that does not accidentally expose a private home/apiary. Clarify payment, preparation time, ready-for-pickup notification, collection window, accessibility, missed pickups, cancellations and returns.

Use the business-owned Facebook/Instagram profiles, approved process/product images, useful local information and QR links to real product pages. Ask for genuine feedback after a completed sale; do not manufacture reviews or condition incentives on positive sentiment.

**Acceptance:** the owner can receive a local order/inquiry, check stock, communicate accurately, prepare it, confirm collection, record payment/sale and reconcile remaining stock. Customers understand where/when to collect before committing.

**Gate:** paid acquisition and new channels wait until this loop works repeatedly and the contribution after labor/fees is understood. Vendor rules, fees, insurance and any product eligibility must be confirmed for each venue.

**Partner questions:** Q05-Q09, Q15, Q17.

## B3 - Put the proven operation online

**Purpose:** make a real local business easier to discover and buy from, without a second conflicting stock ledger.

**Actions:** launch the product-led marketing site with approved real content and contact/social links; then introduce the integrated same-site store when ready. Prefer managed commerce/inventory and hosted checkout. Import real products, batch references where supported, physical stock and fulfillment locations; exclude every demo record.

The catalog, product pages, cart and checkout must agree on price, variant, sellability and pickup eligibility. Record local/in-person sales through the same authority or an explicitly rehearsed reconciliation process. A browser cart does not reserve stock. Staff access uses least privilege/MFA; only genuine operational gaps justify custom admin software.

**Acceptance:** a real test-mode purchase runs through stock validation, receipt, preparation, ready notification, pickup, cancellation/refund and correct stock treatment. The owner performs the workflow without developer intervention.

**Gate:** real payments remain off until inventory, pickup, policy, privacy, tax, platform and owner-training criteria pass. See [commerce phase](phases/04-commerce-and-inventory.md) and [inventory plan](07-inventory-and-operations.md).

**Partner questions:** Q05, Q06, Q10-Q14.

## B4 - Protect the apiary and honey-supply relationship

**Purpose:** avoid promising the same future harvest to both a buyer and retail customers.

**Actions:** privately document the reported meadery arrangement: parties, what "all honey" covers, expected timing, quality/handling/containers, price/payment basis, responsibilities and shortfall/cancellation contingencies. Keep harvest forecasts separate from received physical batches and actual inventory commitments. Review apiary health/capacity planning with suitable local expertise.

Do not turn two hives into a guaranteed harvest forecast. Weather, colony condition, losses and seasonal variability create real supply risk. Do not enter retail honey preorders or an additional pollination commitment against the same assumed output without an explicit capacity/allocation decision.

**Acceptance:** the owners understand the agreement and contingencies, maintain a conservative production/commitment record, and can trace any harvested batch to its disposition. Retail honey stays unavailable unless a real unallocated, approved quantity exists.

**Gate:** invest in apiary expansion only after the owners review experience, workload, colony health, capital needs and buyer/retail demand. No fixed hive-count or yield target is invented here.

**Partner questions:** Q01, Q06, Q16.

## B5 - Add one growth channel at a time

**Purpose:** grow without making fulfillment, quality or cash needs unmanageable.

**Actions:** choose one justified experiment: a proven candle SKU, an accurately stocked gift set, one local wholesale account, or a limited shipping pilot. Define the cost/capacity hypothesis and a stop condition. Test packaging, labeling/channel eligibility, customer support, breakage/returns, shipping rates and applicable obligations before advertising shipping.

Wholesale needs its own price/pack quantities, lead times, payment terms and contribution analysis. Bundles need component stock rules. A new product is not profitable merely because ingredients are cheap; include direct making time and rework.

**Acceptance:** the selected channel earns an acceptable owner-approved contribution, can be fulfilled consistently, and does not compromise current customers or honey commitments.

**Gate:** scale only after evidence from that experiment. Do not launch subscriptions, national shipping, a large wholesale program and new service lines simultaneously.

**Partner questions:** Q06, Q09, Q12-Q16.

## B6 - Evaluate beekeeper services as a separate business line

**Purpose:** avoid presenting technically demanding services as automatically ready because the owners keep bees.

**Actions:** define actual swarm/removal versus structural-work capability, training, insurance, travel, equipment, repair responsibility, permits/contracting requirements where applicable and referral boundaries. For pollination, validate colony capacity/health, transport, site access, crop/timing, grower communications and a suitable agreement.

**Acceptance:** scope, capacity, risk, pricing basis and owner responsibilities are documented and professionally reviewed where needed. Services have a separate lead/scheduling process from product stock.

**Gate:** no instant booking, emergency-response claim or contracted hive supply until the owners can genuinely deliver it. Existing honey allocation and apiary needs must be reconciled with service commitments.

**Partner questions:** Q01, Q11, Q16, Q18.

## Unit economics: a worksheet, not a prediction

Use actual costs for each real SKU:

```text
Net sales per unit = selling price excluding collected tax - discounts/refunds
Variable unit cost = materials + packaging + direct labor + payment/order fees
                   + variable fulfillment + expected waste/returns allowance
Contribution per unit = net sales per unit - variable unit cost
Contribution margin = contribution per unit / net sales per unit
Break-even units for a period = fixed operating cost for that period / contribution per unit
```

Only divide by positive contribution and round required whole-unit targets up. Keep the fixed-cost period and contribution basis consistent. A product with zero/negative contribution needs a pricing, cost or channel decision, not more advertising.

| Example only: not an actual soap price or cost | Illustrative amount |
|---|---:|
| Net selling price, excluding tax | $8.00 |
| Materials | $1.60 |
| Packaging | $0.55 |
| Direct labor allowance | $1.50 |
| Payment/order fee allowance | $0.35 |
| Waste/returns allowance | $0.25 |
| Total variable unit cost | $4.25 |
| Contribution before fixed costs/acquisition | $3.75 |
| Contribution margin | 46.875% |

These numbers are arithmetic examples, **not estimates of this business**, market price research, supplier quotes or a margin target. Pickup handling and customer acquisition must also be included when they create variable costs. Bookkeeping/accounting treatment should be confirmed with a qualified adviser.

Create separate budgets for current production, legal/insurance/recordkeeping, packaging/photos, market fees, website/domain/email, commerce/POS, and later shipping/apiary equipment. The owners must set spending limits, available work time and a contingency reserve privately. No revenue, profit or valuation forecast is asserted.

## First 90 days: proposed cadence, not a deadline promise

| Period | Focus | Evidence before progressing |
|---|---|---|
| First review cycle | B0 decisions, existing soap inventory/formulas/costs, honey-allocation clarification | Owner-approved responsibility/obligation list and honest current-product sheet |
| Following local pilot | B1-B2 repeatable batches, real photos/labels, customer conversations and a safe pickup rehearsal | Completed local transactions, physical counts, actual contribution/capacity observations |
| Following website iteration | Select a concept, publish approved marketing/catalog information, continue operational improvements | Useful customer task outcomes, working contact, no false honey/stock/pickup claims |
| Commerce preparation if ready | B3 platform/stock/pickup setup and test-mode operations | Full commerce gate, not calendar pressure |
| End-of-cycle review | Keep, adjust or stop the chosen growth experiment | Actual costs, repeat demand, fulfillment quality and workload |

Business readiness can precede or outlast website phases. Do not activate selling merely because a visual design is finished.

## Management dashboard

| Measure | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution by SKU/channel | Real net sales minus agreed variable costs | Avoid growing unprofitable sales |
| Repeat purchasing | Actual repeat buyers/orders over a defined period | Distinguish interest from sustained demand |
| Fulfillment reliability | Orders ready/collected within the promised process | Protect local trust and workload |
| Inventory discrepancies | Unexplained differences between physical count and system | Detect missed market sales, damage or process problems |
| Batch/complaint traceability | Ability to locate materials and affected finished units | Support quality response and appropriate recall action |
| Honey commitment coverage | Documented commitments versus conservative forecasts and received/allocated lots | Prevent double-promising; a forecast is not stock |
| Owner time/capacity | Production, customer service, fulfillment, apiary and service hours | Keep growth realistic |

Set targets only after an initial baseline and owner agreement. Page views, social likes and demo-cart additions are not business revenue.
