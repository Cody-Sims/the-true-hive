# Market and design research

Research date: **2026-10-28**. Market focus: **Madisonville, Tennessee**, with clearly separated regional and established national analogues.

## Executive conclusion

The clarified launch strategy is a **locally grounded product-led website with an honest maker story**, supported by distinct service paths. Existing small-scale soap sales and pickup-first fulfillment lead the business plan; a future integrated store must be operationally ready, not a pretend checkout.

**Design recommendation:** compare ten directions and shortlist product-led candidates with the owners. Meadow & Amber is a balanced hypothesis; Fieldwork remains a useful service-forward reference. Hive & Hearth and the new retail directions explore product positioning. None is a proven winner; all require honest demo boundaries and actual local tasks.

No evidence here establishes a universally "best UI," conversion winner, competitor profitability, local market size, search volume, or revenue forecast. Established retail presence is evidence of operating scale, **not proof that a particular design caused success**.

## Method and confidence

Actual public business/directory pages, official framework/platform documentation, and extension publications were read. Competitor findings concern content, navigation labels, calls to action and document structure. **Competitors were not visually audited in a browser or on real phones**; their colors, layout quality, mobile behavior and conversion performance are not asserted.

| Label | Meaning in this plan |
|---|---|
| **Observed** | Directly present in a cited first-party page or official producer directory |
| **Recommended / hypothesis** | Our proposed interpretation or design choice; requires local validation |
| **Unknown** | Not established; must not appear as a business claim or forecast |

Company-provided history, awards, ingredients and testimonials are not independently audited. Directory listings may be stale. Small quotations identify the supporting evidence; competitor copy, photography and branding are not reused in the prototypes.

## Customers and positioning hypotheses

| Segment | Likely decision questions | Proposed page response | What must be researched locally |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homeowners/property managers | Is this honeybee work? Do you cover my town? What happens next? | Clear removal scope, safe assessment guidance, direct contact | Typical inquiry types, structural-work capability, actual travel coverage |
| Farmers/growers | Can you support my crop and bloom window? How do we coordinate? | Separate pollination path and simple scoping information | Demand/timing, hive capacity, transport/access, agreements |
| Local households/gift buyers | What is real/available? Who makes it? Where can I buy locally? | Honest showcase now; real catalog/purchase arrangement later | Product interest, price expectations, pickup/market preferences |
| Social followers | Is this the same business? Can I contact it without an account? | Consistent identity, mobile-friendly sections, direct contact and verified social URLs | Actual audience/channel activity and referral quality |

These are **use-case hypotheses based on the stated business**, not demographic research or proof of willingness to pay. Do not invent affluent demographics, production volumes, local ranking positions or total-addressable-market estimates.

Potential differentiation: one approachable local identity for home/farm services and hive goods; practical service information; real keeper/process imagery; transparent product status; no social-login gate. Each advantage depends on the owner actually being able to deliver it.

## Service-intake evidence

### Removal: distinguish swarm, established colony, and other insects

UT Extension's *Beekeeping in Tennessee*, PB1745, **page 28, "Preparing for a 'Swarm Call'"**, distinguishes honey bees from yellowjackets/hornets and swarms from established colonies. It discusses property location, duration, height, size, disturbance and owner permission, and advises against disturbing/spraying the swarm.

Sources: [publication page](https://solutions.tennessee.edu/publication/PB1745/) and [publisher PDF](https://solutions.tennessee.edu/?view_pub_pdf=723).

**Recommendation:** ask town and what/where the visitor is seeing; allow "unsure." Collect exact property details privately later. Do not ask someone to approach bees for a photo. Swarm collection, structural cut-outs, repair work and extermination are not interchangeable promises.

This publication does **not** verify True Hive's skills, current licenses, insurance, structural obligations, fees, or current legal permission. Current Tennessee requirements were not established by this research.

### Pollination: plan around the farm, not a generic booking slot

UF/IFAS's *Sample Pollination Agreement*, ENY110, discusses timing relative to bloom, colony placement, access, water, contact and pesticide coordination. Relevant topics appear on PDF pages 2-4.

Sources: [publisher record](https://journals.flvc.org/edis/article/view/129086) and [publisher PDF](https://journals.flvc.org/edis/article/download/129086/130432).

**Important limitation:** the publisher identifies this revised-2015 document as **archived**. It is an example of discussion topics, not a current Tennessee contract or operating prescription.

**Recommendation:** first inquiry asks locality, crop, approximate bloom window and contact. Acreage is a reasonable additional scoping hypothesis, not a field specifically established by that agreement. Site access, water, pesticide communication and exact arrangements follow privately. No universal hive-per-acre or yield guarantee is justified.

## Design principles to carry forward

| Principle | Evidence / reasoning | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Place before broad branding | Local directory's concrete locality/contact; confirmed local-first objective | Madisonville in opening content, actual coverage near contact |
| Separate visitor intentions | Big Island Bees' paired actions; distinct extension intake topics | Services visible immediately, removal and farm paths distinct |
| Provenance makes the range coherent | Appalachian Bee and Big Island Bees | Real owner/making story and material explanations |
| Scale navigation to the catalog | National merchants have many collections; True Hive has a small showcase | Four useful primary links, not a megamenu |
| Treat social as a connection, not a dependency | Social links in inspected business pages; template-target risk observed | Verified labeled profile links, direct contact, no required platform account |
| Be explicit about mock products | No live selling has been authorized | Sample prices/counts/cart only in a clearly labeled review; no real checkout or fake offer/rating schema |
| Make trust attributable | First-party stories/physical stores demonstrate context, not independent review evidence | Actual photos/process; no fabricated credentials, counts or reviews |
| Prefer comprehension to motion | Accessibility/performance standards below | HTML-first content, no carousel/autoplay/scroll effects |

## What this research cannot settle

No competitor conversion analytics, authenticated account data, comprehensive provider census, customer interviews, market sizing, pricing survey, keyword-volume data, or legal review was performed. No product formula, credentials, actual capacity, public service radius, shipping policy or social account is verified for The True Hive.
